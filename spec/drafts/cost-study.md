# Cost study: how many labels a `selective_cost` certificate needs (2026-11-12)

**Question.** At the same `alpha` and `delta`, how many labelled cases does a `selective_cost`
certificate (Hoeffding-Bentkus p-value on the mean normalised loss) need, against a
`selective_error` one (v1's exact binomial on the error count)? And is it valid?

**Set-up** (`python/tools/study_cost.py`, seed 20261112, `delta` 0.1). O4's synthetic model
([o4-budget-study.md](o4-budget-study.md)): confidence uniform on [0.5, 1], an error with probability
`beta` (1 - c), `beta` = 4 `alpha` / `k*`, so `k*` is the share of cases the error test could answer
at best. Each error is of the expensive kind with probability `s`; the costs are `r` (expensive) and
1 (cheap), so `c_max` = `r` and the normalised losses are 1 and 1/`r`. Both tests run on the same
temperature and threshold splits of `n_cal` cases each. The grid: `alpha` in {0.05, 0.10}, `k*` in
{0.75, 0.5, 0.25}, `r` in {1, 2, 5, 10}, `s` in {0.2, 0.5, 0.8}, `n_cal` in O4's nine sizes, 648 cells,
1,000 draws per cell and test; then the 4 cells with the highest violation rate at 10,000 draws.
`r` = 1 is the equal-cost case, which the issuer refuses as `selective_cost`; its row shows what the
Hoeffding-Bentkus p-value alone costs.

**Run.** One rented 8-vCPU CPU machine, decision-cert 939b905 (#43), 499 s wall clock. A rerun of the
72 cells at `n_cal` 500 was byte-identical. Output hashes: `rows.json` 05848961f3e38b31...,
`table.md` 4ddc4da44f320154..., `worst.json` 34745c823bdc7fbe...

## Findings

1. **Valid everywhere, with margin.** The largest `selective_cost` violation rate over the 648 cells
   is 0.050 (`delta` is 0.1), and no cell is above 0.1. For `selective_error`, 12 cells are between
   0.10 and 0.118 at 1,000 draws, which is Monte Carlo noise as in O4: at 10,000 draws the four worst
   cells give 0.086 to 0.098 (error) and 0.024 to 0.036 (cost).
2. **The worst draw is above `alpha`, as `delta` allows.** Over the draws that certified, the true
   risk at the chosen threshold exceeds `alpha` in its worst draw in 640 of 640 cells for error and
   482 of 641 for cost: `delta` lets up to 10% of draws land above `alpha`, so in 1,000 draws the worst
   one almost always does. At `n_cal` >= 1,000 the worst draw reaches 1.59 x `alpha` (error) and
   1.43 x (cost); the 90th percentile stays at or below 1.02 x and 0.98 x.
3. **Small tests: an issued certificate is weak evidence.** At `n_cal` <= 300 few draws certify, and
   over only those, the mean risk can be above `alpha`: up to 2.5 x (error) and 2.0 x (cost) at
   `n_cal` 100, where one or two draws in 1,000 certified. The statement is over the calibration
   draw, not conditional on a certificate issuing. v1.4 does not change this; it is the open
   small-test question of the [v1.1 draft's O4](decision-certificate.v1.1.md) and the
   [S1 study](s1-start-study.md).
4. **When cost weighting pays.** `n_cal` at which >= 90% of draws certify:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05, 0.75 | 1,000 | 2,000 | 500 | 500 | 1,000 | 2,000 |
| 0.05, 0.5 | 2,000 | 2,000 | 1,000 | 1,000 | 2,000 | 2,000 |

| 0.10, 0.5 | 1,000 | 1,000 | 500 | 300 | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| 0.10, 0.25 | 2,000 | 2,000 | 1,000 | 1,000 | 2,000 | 2,000 |

- **Cheap errors dominate** (`r` 5-10, `s` 0.2): fewer labels than the error test, and more
  coverage. At `alpha` 0.05, `k*` 0.5, `n_cal` 2,000, the cost test answers 1.99 x `k*` of the cases
  against 0.74 x for the error test.
- **Equal costs** (`r` 1): up to twice the labels for the same claim. This is why the issuer
  writes `selective_error` when costs are equal.
- **Expensive errors dominate** (`s` 0.8): about the `r` = 1 count; little is gained, and the
  Hoeffding-Bentkus penalty remains.

**Recommendation (guidance).** Keep O4's ~1,000-label guidance for v1 (`alpha` 0.05, a strong
model). Use a cost matrix when the cheap kind of error is the common one; expect up to twice O4's
labels when the expensive kind dominates or costs are near equal.
